Sleep on the road, with Belkin

USC studio project in progress, fall 2026. Team of four, in collaboration with Belkin. My role: UX researcher.

Working adults who travel often lose the wind-down routine that helps them sleep at home. We are designing a travel-friendly product that recreates a restful sleep environment anywhere, using affordable, widely available technology and without disturbing the people or space around the user.

  • Concept development
  • User journey mapping
  • Product sketching
Read the case study so far

Who we design for

Working adults aged 25 to 40 who travel for work and leisure, spend a lot of time on screens, and feel unrested even after sleeping enough hours. Their goal is comfortable, uninterrupted sleep anywhere and anytime.

Where we focused

We mapped the evening from setting up the room to falling asleep, and chose the wind-down stage as our solution space. Our solution space is physical and mental relaxation plus natural light exposure, using sight, sound, touch, and scent.

Four concept directions

  • A glasses and eye mask convertible with light-adjusting lenses and bone conduction speakers.
  • Moon (E)scape, a collapsible charging dock that doubles as a night light.
  • A nightstand dock and portable puck with adaptable light and sound.
  • My concept, the Scent Speaker: a compact bedside speaker with a built-in scent diffuser, adjustable scent intensity, and swappable scent cartridges.

Compassionate AI across cultures

USC project in progress, fall 2026. Group of five, in collaboration with Compassion AI.

Generative AI can favor dominant Western perspectives and flatten cultural nuance, so people from diverse cultures often get responses that miss the mark. We are studying what compassionate AI needs to look like for them.

  • Trend research
  • Foresight
  • Cultural inclusion
Read the findings so far

What we found and heard

Compassion is shifting from sounding kind to understanding context and producing meaningful outcomes. People told us that compassion goes beyond empathy, that AI should ask questions before validating or answering, and that it should strengthen human compassion rather than replace it.

Three trends

  • Cultural context is becoming essential.
  • Personalization, including memory, enables cultural relevance.
  • Human judgment stays critical in sensitive areas like healthcare and emotional support.

Three directions we are exploring

AI for people from other cultures living in the US, how major AI companies support users from different cultures, and AI in other countries, including for travelers.

Shoppyist

Browser extension for the startup Shoppyist. Team MatchaCart, five students. My role: UI/UX designer, September 2025 to March 2026.

Companies spend on politics through donations, lobbying, and advocacy, but shoppers rarely see it. Shoppyist shows a brand's score on an issue you care about, right on the shopping page. You can stay on the site or go to a higher-scoring alternative.

Design

  • Merged the score and the alternatives into one panel, so everything is on a single page.
  • Built the Stay/Go idea into a floating badge and a slide-out Go-list.
  • Kept one architecture with a color theme per issue, plus light and dark modes.

Accessibility and ethics

  • Checked contrast with a11y tools and added clearer labels and shadows.

Positivity Perks

Team project at UCI, January to March 2023. Six students. 2nd place at a showcase sponsored by Riot Games, IDEO, and Fair Play Alliance.

Our question: how might we design healthy, inclusive digital spaces where people thrive? We narrowed it to online ranked games, where toxic behavior is common, and designed Positivity Perks, a system that rewards positive communication and teamwork.

  • User research
  • Miro
  • Prototyping
  • Usability testing

View the full project  ·  View Positivity Perks

Read the case study

Research

I ran a literature review on goal alignment, team structure, and matchmaking, and turned it into design principles for our concepts. The team also observed ranked play and ran six semi-structured interviews with game developers, beginning gamers, and players who play as part of their personal identity. We grouped everything into codes and themes with thematic analysis in Miro.

Concepts

We tested four ideas: ranking penalties for poor communication, in-match rewards for helpful communication, filtering hostile language, and design standards for developers. Players preferred concepts that punished poor communication, while developers preferred rewarding good communication. Both groups wanted immediate feedback and a multi-part solution.

Prototype

Positivity Perks grew from storyboards and task flows into paper and low-fidelity prototypes for login, home, rewards, community, and match history pages. We tested it with a think-aloud protocol, then iterated on the themes we found.

Distraction News redesign

Group project at UCI. Team of four.

The children's show Distraction News moves fast, with flashing images and quick cuts. That overwhelms many neurodiverse learners, especially those with ADHD or autism. We redesigned it so viewers control how stimulating it is.

  • Wireframes
  • Storyboards
  • Accessibility
Read the case study

Why it needed to change

The tempo was too fast to follow, sound and visuals came at once, and nothing supported memory or focus. We grounded the critique in Cognitive Load Theory, Mayer's multimedia principles, and Universal Design for Learning, and compared it with BrainPOP and GoNoodle.

What we designed

  • A "Calm Mode" that removes flashing lights and background noise.
  • A "Distraction Level" slider, plus pause, repeat, and playback speed controls.
  • Subtitles in multiple languages, adjustable difficulty, and a digital note-taking area.
  • Before and after wireframes, and a four-panel storyboard of a child using the new version.

Ethics and next steps

We planned A/B testing with neurodiverse learners, with consent, data privacy, and student dignity as priorities. Screen reader support and calm comprehension checks came next.

UCI League GOATS

Project management class at UCI. Team EASYMONEY, five students.

We planned a League of Legends tournament for UCI students and staff to reconnect people with campus through gaming. We chose the game by team vote, scouted the UCI Esports venue, built the budget and rank-based brackets, and made the poster and registration form. The event was not held because we could not secure funding.

Read what we learned

What we produced

A scope statement, Gantt chart, cost estimate, streaming plan for Facebook, Discord, and Twitch, a registration form, and an event poster with a QR code for sign-up.

What we learned

Sponsors were hard to find, and costs ran higher than our first estimate. Next time I would contact sponsors at the start of the project instead of the middle. We also learned to weigh every teammate's opinion and that a problem usually has more than one good answer.
